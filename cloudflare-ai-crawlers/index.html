Is Cloudflare blocking AI crawlers from my website?

Foundash. 18 November 2026. Checked against Cloudflare's documentation.

It can. Cloudflare has a setting called "AI Crawler and Agent access policies" that can block AI search, AI agents and AI training separately. You'll find it in your Cloudflare dashboard under Security > Settings. A one-line test from your computer shows whether it's blocking you right now.

What the setting does

Cloudflare sorts AI traffic into three kinds, and you choose what happens to each (Cloudflare's documentation):

  • Search: crawlers that collect your pages to answer questions about them later.
  • Agent: tools acting for a person in real time, such as ChatGPT fetching a page someone asked about.
  • Training: crawlers taking your pages to train a model.

For each one you can block it, block it only on pages with ads, or allow it. For Training there is also a "Disallow" option, which asks crawlers to stay away through your robots.txt.

If Search or Agent is set to block, assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity can't read your pages when a customer asks about you. Blocking only Training does not stop them recommending you.

Is it on by default?

Cloudflare's documentation says new domains allow all three by default (read 18 November 2026). But a setting someone chose earlier stays in place.

It happened to us. In August 2026 our own site on Cloudflare answered GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot with a 403, and its robots.txt told eight AI crawlers to stay out. Nobody here wrote either rule. They came from a Cloudflare setting on the account (the full story).

How to check

  1. In the Cloudflare dashboard, open your domain and go to Security > Settings. Filter by "Bot traffic" and open "AI Crawler and Agent access policies". Check the action for Search, Agent and Training.
  2. Test your site from outside with ChatGPT's real crawler names (see How to check if your website is blocking ChatGPT). Cloudflare applies these policies to requests that match a bot's name, not only to verified bots, so the test shows what the setting does.
  3. Open yoursite.com/robots.txt and look for a section Cloudflare added, often with a Content-Signal line. Cloudflare's managed robots.txt refuses training crawlers and leaves search crawlers alone (N&D Co. found this in its November 2026 study).

How common is this?

  • Cloudflare's managed robots.txt is behind most robots.txt blocks on small business sites. A study of 4,564 small business websites found 105 of the 154 sites blocking an AI crawler used it (N&D Co., November 2026).
  • A study of 3,693 small business websites found 5.8% showed its crawler a bot check instead of the homepage, Cloudflare's for 3.0% (Preferly, October 2026).
  • In our own check of 293 local business websites, 4 of the 12 sites that refused an AI crawler without any robots.txt rule were behind Cloudflare. The rest ran on five other setups (Foundash research, August 2026).

Other Cloudflare features that can turn crawlers away

Bot protection and custom security rules can challenge or block automated visitors in general, AI crawlers included. If the AI policy looks right but the test in step 2 still shows a 403, look there next.
