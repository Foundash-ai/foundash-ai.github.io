How many small business websites block AI crawlers?

Foundash. 18 November 2026. 293 local business websites in Fairfax, Loudoun and Arlington, Virginia, checked in August 2026.

About 1 in 16 in our check. 18 of 293 local business websites refused an AI crawler while letting browsers and Google in, and 12 of those 18 had no rule anywhere saying to block AI. The owners most likely never chose it.

Of the 18 server refusals, 11 answered with a 403. The other 7 answered with a 429 ("too many requests") that held on every re-test, while a browser got a normal page from the same address moments later.

What it means for a business

A training block (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot) is usually a choice, and it does not stop an assistant recommending you.

A server refusal with no rule behind it is different. Something between the crawler and the site turns it away, usually without the owner seeing it: a CDN, a host's bot protection or a security plugin. The 12 sites like that ran on six different setups: 4 behind Cloudflare, 3 on nginx (all three identifying as WP Engine), 2 on LiteSpeed, 1 on Apache, 1 behind a CDN calling itself "hcdn", and 1 that sent no server name at all.

The other two studies are much larger, and their robots.txt numbers are the better guide to how often businesses opt out on purpose. Ours answers a narrower question: how often a site turns an AI crawler away while letting everyone else in. Because each refusal is tested against those controls, it is aimed at AI crawlers specifically.

Our robots.txt rate (5.1%) is higher than theirs, partly because six dental practices in our sample share the same file.

Method

We took 310 business websites from OpenStreetMap across Fairfax, Loudoun and Arlington: dentists, hairdressers, car repair, vets, HVAC, plumbers and roofers. 293 answered. Each site was requested with each crawler's real user agent string, with a normal browser, Googlebot, an unknown visitor and curl as controls. Every refusal was re-tested one request at a time, three seconds apart. The full method, and the mistakes we made along the way, are in the research write-up.
